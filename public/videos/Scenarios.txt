1. Main scenario: Future plantation expansion is allocated hierarchically, with expansion first occurring within the country to which the plantation demand is assigned. Within each country, semi-natural forest areas closest to existing plantations are converted first, allowing new plantations to expand around the existing plantation distribution. If the available semi-natural forest area within a country is insufficient to meet its annual plantation requirement, the remaining area is successively allocated within the corresponding United Nations subregion, United Nations region and, if necessary, globally. At each spatial level, areas closest to existing plantations are prioritised until the required plantation expansion is achieved.

2. Homogeneous allocation within United Nations subregions: In this sensitivity scenario, future plantation expansion is distributed homogeneously across eligible semi-natural forest areas within the corresponding United Nations subregion. Unlike the main scenario, allocation does not prioritise areas based on their distance from existing plantations. Plantation expansion is therefore distributed across the available semi-natural forest area within each United Nations subregion, providing a spatially dispersed alternative to the clustered expansion represented in the main scenario.

3. Seeded cluster expansion within United Nations regions: In this sensitivity scenario, future plantation expansion is represented as spatially clustered development within United Nations regions. Country level plantation demand was aggregated within each United Nations region and allocated within the corresponding region. Seed locations were selected separately within each region from existing plantation cells located near semi-natural forests. Spatially separated seed locations were randomly selected, with selection weighted by existing plantation area. The number of seeds was scaled to the semi-natural forest area within each region. Regional plantation demand was then allocated around these seeds within the corresponding United Nations region, producing spatially clustered areas of new plantation forest.

4. Global seeded cluster expansion: In this sensitivity scenario, future plantation expansion is represented as spatially clustered development at the global scale. Country level plantation demand was aggregated globally and allocated without country or regional boundaries. Seed locations were selected separately within United Nations regions from existing plantation cells located near semi-natural forests. Spatially separated seed locations were randomly selected, with selection weighted by existing plantation area. The number of seeds was scaled to the semi-natural forest area within each region. Total global plantation demand was then allocated around these seeds across all eligible areas globally, producing spatially clustered areas of new plantation forest.
